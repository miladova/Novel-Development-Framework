# THE PRICE OF A NAME — Working Outline

**Setting:** Rome, spring–autumn AD 112, with flashbacks to AD 86–88 and an epilogue in AD 118.
**Rating:** Mature (18+). Adult themes treated with literary seriousness: sexual exploitation, slavery, violence, grief, political corruption. No gratuitous explicitness.
**Length target:** 25 chapters + epilogue, 5,000–6,000 words each.

---

## Principal cast

**Household of the Golden Vine (Esquiline, Rome)**
- **Antonia Cassia**, 41. Born a slave in Baiae. Freed, then rich. Owner of the Golden Vine. Sharp, generous, merciless when required.
- **Myrrhine**, 36, Greek freedwoman; steward of the house; Antonia's oldest ally and once her lover.
- **Cinnamus**, 54, freedman atriensis (household manager); keeps the accounts and the secrets.
- **Eucharis**, 25, Greek, the house's most celebrated woman; harpist; saving to buy her own freedom.
- **Prima**, 30, Roman-born, the house's longest-serving woman; dangerous, clear-eyed, unillusioned.
- **Lucilla**, 17, newly purchased; a singer with a remarkable voice; the household's conscience and its vulnerability.
- **Meno**, 60s, Thracian doorkeeper. **Pamphilus**, physician. **Brennus**, a Sequani freedman, guard.

**The Tarquitii (ancient Etruscan lineage, the Palatine)**
- **Lucius Tarquitius Priscus**, 53, senator, consul suffect AD 97, jurist, candidate for the proconsulship of Asia. The man from Antonia's past.
- **Tarquitia Priscilla**, 78, his mother; blind; the family's memory.
- **Tarquitia Marcellina**, 19, his daughter; devout, observant, secretly starving for a life of her own.
- **Servius Aelius Gracilis**, 27, her betrothed; old name, new debts.
- **Vibia Lucana**, 45, Tarquitius's cousin; widow; cynical, funny, Antonia's unlikely friend.

**Elsewhere in the city**
- **Gaius Cluvius Naso**, 61, banker and merchant; Antonia's partner and creditor.
- **Marcus Sestius Verus**, 38, senator; a guest of the house; a man who takes what he is not refused.
- **Aulus Rubrius Celer**, 26, equestrian, newly a senator; Antonia's client and protégé.
- **Tertia**, 25, a scribe and accountant, hired late in the book. (The child Antonia was told had died.)
- **Bassa**, 70, Antonia's old nurse, living at Baiae.
- **The delator** **Hilarus**, a professional informer.

**Offstage:** Trajan; Plotina; the machinery of the Ulpian building works.

---

## Structure

### PART ONE — THE HOUSE
1. **The Golden Vine.** A banquet for the opening of the new dining room. The world, the household, the guests, the economy of favours. Antonia negotiates with Cluvius Naso. A guest's cruelty to Lucilla. At midnight a sealed tablet arrives from Tarquitius Priscus.
2. **What the House Requires.** Antonia's morning: petitions, wages, the sick, the purchase of Lucilla, a visit from Rubrius Celer. The news breaks that Tarquitius is to be curator of the city's building works and a candidate for Asia — power over her permits, her water, her walls. Antonia's rule: never be surprised in public.
3. **The Weight of a Name.** Tarquitius at home: the morning salutatio, his mother's silence, his daughter's betrothal, his debts of honour. His contempt for Antonia — and the fact that he cannot stop thinking about her. Ends with his decision to send the second tablet.
4. **Debts Called In.** Cluvius Naso calls in a loan of two million sesterces. Antonia's counter-move: a wager on the grain market, a favour owed by Vibia Lucana, a secret she has kept for eleven years. Ends with her decision to go to the Tarquitii's house herself.
5. **The Linen Room (AD 86).** Flashback: Cassia, 15, sold into the household at Arretium; the linen room; Lucius, 27, home from the Danube; the beginning.
6. **The Price of a Girl (AD 87–88).** Flashback: the affair discovered; pregnancy; the birth in a slave infirmary; the child taken; the sale to the brothel at Puteoli. Ends with the first line Antonia ever spoke to a free man as property.

### PART TWO — THE PRICE OF A NAME
7. **The Informer.** Antonia hires Hilarus to find a child sold out of a house at Arretium twenty-four years before. Parallel: Tarquitius's political morning, the prosecution of a corrupt governor, his need for a witness only Antonia can supply.
8. **A Daughter of the House.** Marcellina comes to the Golden Vine in disguise, alone, at dusk. She wants to know what her father will not tell her. Antonia sees the girl's hunger and uses it — and is ashamed.
9. **The Dedication.** Public spectacle: games and a dedication in the Forum of Trajan. Antonia and Tarquitius meet in front of the whole city. The scene where the past is visible to anyone who knows how to look.
10. **The Dog Days.** The house in August: heat, a dinner that turns; Sestius Verus and Lucilla; the limits of Antonia's power. A senator's son dies in an upper room. The house must decide what story it will tell.
11. **Two Kinds of Silence.** Tarquitius summons Antonia. The negotiation: he needs her ledger of a dead governor's debts; she needs his protection from the dead boy's family. Neither yields. The first honest conversation in twenty-four years, and it is not about the truth.
12. **The Ledger of Favours.** Antonia's counterstrike: she opens a door for Vibia Lucana and closes one on Sestius. Myrrhine's warning. The first crack in the household.
13. **Baiae.** Bassa is dying. Antonia returns to the town she was sold from; the sea, the old woman's confession, the name of a midwife. The first proof that the child may have lived.

### PART THREE — WHAT THE STREETS KNOW
14. **The Secretary.** Tertia is hired: a widow, a scribe, precise, proud, with a habit of counting under her breath. Antonia does not know why she cannot stop looking at her.
15. **The Old Woman's Confession.** Antonia goes to Tarquitia Priscilla. The truth of the transaction of AD 88: who gave the order, who disobeyed it, who has known for twenty-four years.
16. **The Delator.** Hilarus brings a charge: Antonia is accused of poisoning Marcus Sestius's son and of keeping a disorderly house. The machinery of law begins to turn. Tarquitius is named as her patron's enemy — and as her only possible advocate.
17. **Blood in the Courtyard.** Prima's revenge on Sestius; Lucilla's flight; a death in the alley behind the house. Antonia chooses what to bury and what to confess.
18. **A House Divided.** Tarquitius and Marcellina; the betrothal breaks; the daughter's rage; the senator's discovery that Tertia exists. He goes to look at her from across a street.
19. **The Sealing of the Doors.** The magistrate's seal on the Golden Vine. The women sent to friends, to the country, to worse places. Antonia alone in an emptied house, and Myrrhine's choice.

### PART FOUR — THE JUDGEMENT OF THE CITY
20. **The Women of the Vine.** Antonia assembles her defence: Eucharis, Prima, Vibia Lucana, Cluvius Naso, Cinnamus's ledgers. The price of each testimony.
21. **The Prosecution.** The basilica. Sestius's speech: a woman, a freedwoman, a brothel, a dead boy. The city watching. Antonia's own answer.
22. **The Defence.** Tarquitius speaks for her. What he says, and what he does not say. The courtroom learns nothing and suspects everything.
23. **The Verdict.** Acquittal, and the cost: the emperor's condition, the loss of the licence, the ruin of Sestius's career or Tarquitius's. Marcellina's choice. Tertia's question.
24. **Fire.** The Esquiline burns. The house, the ledgers, the women, the little shrine. What Antonia carries out and what she leaves.

### PART FIVE — THE RECKONING
25. **The Reckoning.** Antonia and Tarquitius, alone, in the ruins. The past spoken aloud for the first time. Not forgiveness: settlement. The last bargain.
**Epilogue — AD 118.** The house rebuilt as something else; the women dispersed into their lives; Tertia's name on a stone; Antonia at fifty, free, rich, and not accepted. Tarquitius's death in the east, and the letter. The last line returns to the tagline: power is inherited, fortunes are made, some wounds never heal.
